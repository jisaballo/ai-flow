# decisions-global.md

**What it is.** `decisions-global.md` is the record of decisions that cross tasks, with the context and
the alternatives that keep them from being re-argued. Its shape, how it is read, written and kept, are the
[context mechanism](context.md)'s and are not restated here. This document states what is the decision
log's alone: that a section is a decision, what its nano line carries, its place in the engine as reader
and writer, and its one exception to the shape.

## A section is a decision

Each `##` heading is one decision, dated and titled, holding its context, the decision, and the
alternatives considered with the reason each was rejected. The nano line carries **the decision itself** in
one sentence — not its context — because the line is what decides whether the body is opened. A decision's
title carries no task ID; where an epic or task is the decision's occasion, the context names it, as
provenance.

## Who reads it

Its nano is in every task's context set. A section loads when the task's ground touches the decision —
when the task rests on it or would reopen it — by the same intersection the Icebox scan makes over an
entry's statement. That is the whole of its reader: a task about to re-decide something finds the decision
that already settled it, before it spends a round re-arguing it.

## Who writes it

The archive checklist, from the plan. A Decision Register entry the operator approved and the plan marked
**global** — a decision that reaches beyond the task, on the grounds the Plan protocol gives layer 2 —
becomes a section at the close, in the file's format: context, decision, alternatives. A decision that
concerns the task alone stays in the task's papers and its summary, and never reaches this file. The
operator writes it by hand for a decision that no task carried.

## The exception to the shape

A decision is an essay by nature: the alternatives and their reasons are the content that keeps it from
being re-argued, and a decision that omits them is the one that gets reopened. The section length the check
applies to every other class does not apply to a decision's body. The nano line's length applies in full,
since the line is what every task reads.

**The section count does not apply either, and for a different reason.** Every `##` here is one decision,
so this file's section count *is* its decision count — it rises with every close that mints one, and
nothing in the mechanism retires a decision. A ceiling over it could only ever be crossed, which is why
the count bounds topics and not records ([the mechanism](context.md) states the rule). Both exemptions are
expressed the same way: the check draws **no verdict at all** for them, rather than a passing one. A file
of many decisions is not out of shape; it is a file that has been used.
